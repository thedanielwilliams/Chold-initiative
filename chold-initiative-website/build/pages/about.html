About us

A non-profit built for the whole livestock system, not just one part of it.

CHOLD Initiative exists because livestock development in Africa keeps failing at the seams: between data and decision, between veterinary science and community trust, between national policy and the grazing route. We work on the seams.

Our story

From communication for development to livestock systems change.

The Center for Holistic Livestock Development Initiative (CHOLD Initiative) is a forward-looking non-profit organisation dedicated to transforming livestock ecosystems through innovation, research, data intelligence and inclusive leadership engagement.

We were founded in 2018 and duly registered with the Corporate Affairs Commission of Nigeria under the Companies and Allied Matters Act 1990 as Cambio Development Communication Initiative. In 2025 the organisation was renamed CHOLD Initiative to reflect a sharpened focus on the livestock sector and the systems that surround it.

We focus on the holistic development of livestock systems by integrating cutting-edge technologies with traditional practices, fostering sustainable livelihoods and strengthening value chains. Our work empowers communities, promotes animal health and creates resilient food systems, without boundaries or limitations of geography.

CHOLD Initiative bridges the gap between tradition and innovation, ensuring livestock remains a reliable asset for development, peace and prosperity.

“Empowering livestock communities, securing Africa's future.”

That sentence is the whole brief. Everything we build (a database, a surveillance network, a training curriculum, a conversation with an Emir) is judged against whether it leaves a livestock-keeping household more secure than it found them.

Our focus

National livestock development.

Livestock is a vital component of Nigeria's agricultural sector, contributing approximately 5–8% to national GDP and supporting the livelihoods of over 20 million people, especially in rural and pastoral communities. The country hosts a diverse livestock population (cattle, sheep, goats, poultry and pigs) which plays critical roles in food security, income generation and cultural practice.

Despite its importance, the sector faces low productivity, limited access to veterinary services, weak data systems, poor infrastructure, disease outbreaks, climate change and recurring farmer–herder conflicts. In response, the Federal Government has launched initiatives including the National Livestock Transformation Plan (NLTP), Animal Disease Control Programmes, and the Livestock Productivity and Resilience Support (L-PRES) project.

With strategic investment, innovation and inclusive governance, livestock development holds vast potential to drive rural transformation, nutrition and economic diversification in Nigeria and across the continent.

Our understanding of the challenges

Eight constraints holding the sector back.

We name these plainly because a development programme that misdiagnoses the constraint will spend years solving the wrong problem.

01

Low productivity & poor genetics

Milk yield among indigenous cows is around 5 litres a day against 15+ litres in dairy breeds, leading Nigeria to import roughly 60% of its milk (~900,000 tonnes) at a cost of about $1.5 billion a year.

02

Animal disease & weak surveillance

Outbreaks of diseases like FMD, PPR and African Swine Fever continue due to insufficient veterinary services, poor biosecurity and weak monitoring systems.

03

Inadequate infrastructure & logistics

Poor roads, lack of cold storage, insufficient abattoirs and veterinary clinics lead to high transit losses and low market access.

04

Farmer–herder conflict & insecurity

Desertification and shrinking grazing lands drive cattle south, leading to violent clashes, causing 8,343 deaths in conflicts from 2005–2021. In Zamfara alone, around 377,245 livestock were stolen between 2011 and 2019, amounting to over ₦2.8 billion in losses.

05

Climate change & environmental degradation

Northern Nigeria loses around 350,000 hectares of land annually to desertification, costing about $5.1 billion per year. Overgrazing and deforestation degrade rangelands and reduce pasture availability.

06

Limited access to finance & veterinary services

Smallholders struggle to access credit, vaccines, quality feed and professional veterinary care.

07

Weak data systems & technical capacity

Around 77% of livestock farmers in 16 states plus the FCT lack basic production records. Low adoption of modern practices persists due to scarce technical know-how.

08

Low engagement of key stakeholders

Critical actors and gatekeepers are not strategically engaged in livestock development in Nigeria. Institutions like the traditional institutions (with proximity and direct influence on communities) have only been engaged passively.
