Who we are

Bridging the gap between tradition and innovation.

The Center for Holistic Livestock Development Initiative is a forward-looking non-profit dedicated to transforming livestock ecosystems through innovation, research, data intelligence and inclusive leadership engagement.

Founded in 2018 and registered with the Corporate Affairs Commission of Nigeria under the Companies and Allied Matters Act, we focus on the holistic development of livestock systems — integrating cutting-edge technology with traditional practice, fostering sustainable livelihoods and strengthening value chains. Our work empowers communities, promotes animal health and creates resilient food systems, without boundaries or limitations of geography.

The challenge

Livestock underpins the economy. The systems around it do not yet match its weight.

Livestock contributes roughly 5–8% of Nigeria's GDP and supports the livelihoods of over 20 million people. Yet the sector is held back by weak data, fragile animal health systems, degraded rangelands and conflict.

01

Low productivity & poor genetics

Indigenous cows yield around 5 litres a day against 15+ litres in dairy breeds, pushing Nigeria to import roughly 60% of its milk — some 900,000 tonnes at a cost near $1.5 billion a year.

02

Disease & weak surveillance

Outbreaks of FMD, PPR and African Swine Fever persist because veterinary services are thin, biosecurity is weak and monitoring systems are fragmented.

03

Inadequate infrastructure

Poor roads, absent cold storage and insufficient abattoirs and veterinary clinics drive high transit losses and lock producers out of formal markets.

04

Conflict & insecurity

Shrinking grazing land drove 8,343 conflict deaths between 2005 and 2021. In Zamfara alone some 377,245 animals were stolen between 2011 and 2019 — over ₦2.8 billion in losses.

05

Climate & land degradation

Northern Nigeria loses around 350,000 hectares to desertification each year, costing an estimated $5.1 billion annually and steadily reducing available pasture.

06

Weak data & passive engagement

Around 77% of livestock farmers across 16 states and the FCT keep no basic production records — and the traditional institutions closest to those communities have only ever been engaged passively.

What we do

Eight technical capabilities, one connected system.

We deploy a systems-thinking framework that combines technical innovation with grassroots engagement — because a vaccine campaign without data, or a database without the trust of traditional leaders, will not hold.

Livestock Information Management Systems

Digital and field-integrated systems that collect, store, analyse and visualise real-time data on populations, ownership, health status, production and movement — with GIS and IoT integration.

Animal Disease Surveillance

Integrated surveillance built on grassroots informants, mobile reporting, veterinary partnerships and emergency dashboards to detect and respond to zoonotic and transboundary disease.

Traditional Leader Engagement

Emirs, Obas, chiefs, religious leaders and local councils as full development allies in vaccination drives, sustainable grazing, peacebuilding and gender-inclusive programming.

Livestock Research & Innovation

Applied and participatory research on animal health, genetics, feed systems, climate adaptation, indigenous practice and digital technology — generating homegrown solutions.

Livestock Business Analytics

Production, cost and market data turned into insight for policy, private investment and smallholder profitability — including predictive dashboards for feed prices and demand.

Supply Chain Management

Mapping and digitising meat, dairy and by-product chains from production through cold storage and certification — with blockchain traceability piloted from farm to fork.

Emergency Coordination Centres & Labs

Designing and supporting ECCs for rapid animal health response, alongside biosecure laboratories for diagnostics, vaccine trials and antimicrobial resistance research.

Government System Strengthening

Technical assistance to MDAs — FMARD, NVRI, NALDA, VCN and their state counterparts — on planning, data management, M&E and policy harmonisation.
Strategic approach

Five pillars that hold the work together.

PILLAR 01

Livestock intelligence

Designing and deploying livestock information management systems, with real-time analytics for disease surveillance and productivity tracking.

PILLAR 02

Research & development

Applied research on context-specific challenges, promoting indigenous knowledge alongside evidence-based innovation.

PILLAR 03

Traditional leadership

Empowering traditional leaders as development allies and bridging cultural governance with scientific advancement.

PILLAR 04

Value chain strengthening

Supporting market access, infrastructure and supply chain efficiency while promoting local agribusiness and entrepreneurship.

PILLAR 05

Capacity building

Training stakeholders in animal health, data use and system design, and building institutional capability at national, state and grassroots levels.
